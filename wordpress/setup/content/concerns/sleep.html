Why women sleep worse (and it's not in your head)

Women are more likely than men to report insomnia, and hormones are a big part of why. Progesterone has a calming effect, so when it drops before your period, sleep often gets lighter and more broken. Add stress, a racing mind at bedtime, late screens and a too-warm bedroom, and you get the classic pattern: exhausted all day, wide awake at night.

A 20-minute wind-down that makes any supplement work better

  1. Dim the lights an hour before bed. Bright light suppresses your natural melatonin.
  2. Take your Evenly Unwind and make it a ritual: tea, a shower, a book.
  3. Brain dump: write tomorrow's to-do list on paper so it isn't looping in your head.
  4. Keep the bedroom cool, around 65–68°F.
  5. Same wake-up time daily, even on weekends. That anchors your body clock more than bedtime does.

When to talk to a doctor

If you've had trouble sleeping three or more nights a week for three months, if you snore loudly or wake up gasping, or if you're very sleepy during the day, please see a doctor. Chronic insomnia and sleep apnea are common and treatable. Supplements are best for occasional sleeplessness, not a substitute for care.
