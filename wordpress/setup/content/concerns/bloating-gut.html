Where bloating actually comes from

Most everyday bloating comes from one of three things:

  • Food that isn't fully broken down. What your body doesn't digest higher up gets fermented by gut bacteria lower down, and fermentation means gas.
  • A gut microbiome that's off balance after antibiotics, stress, travel or a run of takeout.
  • Hormones. Many women bloat noticeably in the week before their period, from water retention and slower digestion.

Eating fast, fizzy drinks, chewing gum and sugar alcohols (sorbitol, xylitol, often in "sugar-free" snacks) make all of it worse.

Five habits that ease bloating fast

  1. Slow down. Put your fork down between bites. Swallowed air is a big, underrated cause of bloating.
  2. Walk for 10 minutes after meals. Gentle movement helps move gas along.
  3. Check the "sugar-free" labels for sorbitol, mannitol and xylitol.
  4. Swap fizzy drinks for still water or peppermint or ginger tea.
  5. Keep a food-and-symptom log for two weeks. Patterns jump out fast.

When bloating needs a doctor

Occasional bloating is normal. But see your doctor if it's there most days for several weeks, especially if you're over 50, or if it comes with weight loss you can't explain, blood in your stool, vomiting, a lasting change in bowel habits, getting full very quickly or pelvic pain. Persistent bloating is one of the symptoms doctors check for celiac disease and ovarian cancer, so it's worth getting looked at.
