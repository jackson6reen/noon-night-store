Why your period hurts (in plain English)

In the days around your period, the lining of your uterus releases prostaglandins, hormone-like compounds that make the uterine muscle contract. More prostaglandins mean stronger contractions, and that's the cramping, aching feeling low in your belly and back. Prostaglandins can also reach your gut, which is why many women get loose stools or gas on day one.

Meanwhile, the drop in estrogen and progesterone before your period can bring on the rest of the package: water retention and bloating, breast tenderness, irritability and broken sleep. It's real, it's common, and you're allowed to want support for it.

Habits that make supplements work better

  • Start before it starts. Supplements work best taken daily across your whole cycle, not just on day one.
  • Heat helps. A heating pad on your lower belly is a well-studied, drug-free way to ease cramps.
  • Move gently. Walking and light stretching boost blood flow and can ease cramping.
  • Go easy on salt and alcohol in the week before your period to limit water retention.
  • Track your cycle so you can see what's actually changing over 2–3 months.

When period pain is a sign to see a doctor

Painful periods are common, but severe pain isn't something to just live with. Please talk to a doctor if your pain keeps you from daily life, doesn't respond to over-the-counter pain relievers, has gotten worse over the years, or comes with very heavy bleeding, pain during sex or bleeding between periods. Conditions like endometriosis and fibroids are common, under-diagnosed and treatable. Supplements can support everyday comfort; they're not a substitute for a diagnosis.
